About Me

I'm a PhD student in Cognitive Neuroscience in the Department of Experimental Psychology at the University of Granada (CIMCYC), supervised by Professors María Ruz and Christopher Summerfield (University of Oxford). My doctoral research explores the relationship between spatial memory and category learning, investigating how spatial representations support more abstract forms of cognition through behavioral, computational, and neuroimaging approaches.

My work combines experimental design, functional neuroimaging (fMRI), machine learning, and computational modeling, including artificial neural networks. More broadly, I'm interested in the intersection of neuroscience and artificial intelligence, particularly how humans and artificial systems learn, represent information, and generalize knowledge.

Before starting my PhD, I earned dual Bachelor's degrees in Biomedical Engineering and Chemical & Biological Engineering from Colorado State University, with a minor in Mathematics. My previous research and professional experience spans diffusion MRI and brain aging, public health research, 3D fluorescence microscopy, and applied data science in a SaaS environment. This interdisciplinary background continues to shape how I approach research and problem-solving.
